Why Did My Escrow Go Up? A Texas Homeowner's Guide

If you're a homeowner in Katy, Houston, or anywhere across Texas, you've probably opened your mortgage statement and noticed something unsettling: your escrow payment just went up. Again. You're not alone in feeling confused or frustrated. Escrow increases are one of the most common questions we hear from homeowners, and understanding why they happen is the first step toward managing your mortgage more confidently.

An escrow account holds funds for your property taxes, homeowners insurance, and sometimes mortgage insurance or HOA fees. Your lender collects these payments monthly as part of your mortgage payment, then disburses them on your behalf when they're due. Sounds straightforward, right? The tricky part is that these costs aren't fixed—they change, and when they do, your escrow payment often follows.

The Main Reasons Your Escrow Payment Increased

Rising Property Tax Assessments

In Texas, property taxes are a significant part of homeownership costs. When your local county appraisal district reassesses your home's value (which happens regularly), your property tax bill can jump substantially. This is especially common in growing areas like Katy and the greater Houston metropolitan area, where property values have appreciated considerably.

Your mortgage servicer reviews your escrow account annually and adjusts your monthly payment to ensure sufficient funds are set aside for the coming year. If your tax assessment went up, so does your escrow payment. It's a direct correlation, and unfortunately, there's not much you can do to prevent it—though you may have the right to protest your assessment through your county's formal process.

Increased Homeowners Insurance Premiums

Insurance costs have been rising across Texas in recent years. Whether due to increased claims in your area, inflation, or changes in your home's risk profile, your homeowners insurance premium can increase significantly from one renewal period to the next. When your escrow servicer calculates next year's costs, they account for these new premiums.

Unlike property taxes, you have more control here. Shopping around for better rates, increasing your deductible, or bundling policies with the same insurer can help lower your premium—and ultimately, your escrow payment.

Escrow Shortage from the Previous Year

Sometimes, your lender's initial escrow estimate is too low. If the actual costs for property taxes or insurance exceeded what was collected during the year, you'll have a shortage. Your servicer addresses this by increasing your monthly escrow payment to recover that deficit over time. This is a common occurrence and isn't a cause for alarm—it's simply a correction of the estimate.

PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance) Adjustments

If your loan-to-value ratio hasn't improved enough to remove PMI, or if you're carrying a mortgage with PMI, changes to mortgage insurance costs can affect your escrow account. Texas borrowers putting down less than 20% typically carry PMI, which is escrowed along with taxes and insurance.

HOA Fee Increases (If Applicable)

Many neighborhoods in the Katy and Houston areas include HOA fees, which may be escrowed. If your HOA raises dues, you'll see that reflected in your escrow payment.

How to Review Your Escrow Statement

Your mortgage servicer is required to send you an annual escrow account statement. Take time to review it carefully. Look for:

  • The opening balance of your escrow account
  • Actual disbursements made (taxes, insurance, PMI)
  • Projected costs for the coming year
  • Any shortage or surplus calculation
  • Your new monthly escrow payment amount

If the numbers seem off or you don't understand the increase, contact your servicer and ask for clarification. It's your right as a borrower to fully understand where your money is going.

What Can You Do About Rising Escrow?

Contest Your Property Tax Assessment

In Texas, you can file a formal protest with your county appraisal district if you believe your home's assessed value is too high. The deadline is typically around May 15th each year. If successful, you could lower your property taxes and subsequent escrow payment.

Shop for Better Insurance Rates

Don't assume your current homeowners insurance rate is competitive. Get quotes from multiple insurers annually. A lower premium directly reduces your escrow payment.

Review Your Coverage Needs

Speak with your insurance agent about whether you're over-insured. Adjusting coverage levels or increasing your deductible might lower premiums—though always maintain adequate protection.

Accelerate Equity Building

If PMI is part of your escrow, building equity faster gets you closer to removing it. Making extra principal payments can help you reach the 20% equity threshold sooner, which eliminates PMI and reduces your escrow obligation.

Should You Refinance?

Some homeowners explore refinancing when escrow increases significantly. A refinance can sometimes improve your loan terms or help you tap into home equity. However, refinancing involves closing costs and a new loan process, so it's important to weigh those factors carefully. Understanding Your Options as a Texas Homeowner

Escrow increases feel sudden and frustrating, but they're a normal part of homeownership in Texas. Property taxes and insurance costs evolve, and your mortgage payment adjusts accordingly. The key is understanding why the increase happened and knowing what levers you can pull—whether that's contesting your tax assessment, shopping for insurance, or exploring refinancing options.
