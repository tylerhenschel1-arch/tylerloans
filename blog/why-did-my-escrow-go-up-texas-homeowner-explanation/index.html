Why Did My Escrow Go Up? A Texas Homeowner's Guide

If you're a homeowner in the Katy or Houston area and you've recently opened your mortgage statement to find that your escrow payment has increased, you're not alone. Many Texas homeowners are surprised and confused when their monthly mortgage payment jumps without warning. The culprit? An escrow adjustment.

Understanding why your escrow went up is essential to managing your home loan responsibly. In this guide, we'll walk through exactly what escrow is, why adjustments happen, and what you can do about it.

What Is Escrow, Anyway?

Your monthly mortgage payment typically consists of four components, often remembered by the acronym PITI:

  • Principal — the amount you borrowed
  • Interest — the cost of borrowing
  • Taxes — your property tax obligation
  • Insurance — homeowners insurance and mortgage insurance (if applicable)

The escrow account is where your mortgage servicer holds money for your property taxes and homeowners insurance. Rather than paying these bills directly yourself, you pay a portion of the estimated annual costs each month as part of your mortgage payment. Your servicer then pays the taxes and insurance on your behalf when they're due.

This system protects both you and your lender. It ensures that your property taxes get paid (which protects the lender's collateral) and that your home remains insured.

Why Escrow Amounts Increase

Property Tax Increases

In Texas, property taxes are a significant factor in escrow calculations. If your home's assessed value increases or your local tax rate rises, your escrow payment will increase to account for the higher annual tax bill. This is one of the most common reasons Houston and Katy homeowners see escrow adjustments.

Homeowners Insurance Premium Increases

Insurance companies regularly adjust their rates based on claims history, inflation, and local market conditions. If your homeowners insurance premium goes up, your escrow payment will rise proportionally. Texas homeowners have experienced notable insurance increases in recent years, making this a frequent source of escrow adjustments.

Escrow Account Shortage

Your servicer conducts an annual escrow analysis to compare what they actually paid for taxes and insurance against what they collected from you. If there's a shortage — meaning they paid out more than they collected — they'll increase your monthly payment to cover the difference and build a proper cushion.

Changes in Your Loan Amount or Rate

While this is less common, certain loan modifications or refinancing scenarios can affect escrow calculations. Additionally, if you had mortgage insurance (PMI) that recently fell off your loan, your overall payment structure may have shifted.

Understanding Your Escrow Analysis Statement

When your servicer increases your escrow payment, they should send you an escrow analysis statement. This document breaks down:

  • Your estimated annual property taxes
  • Your estimated annual homeowners insurance premium
  • The new monthly escrow amount
  • Any shortage or surplus from the prior year

Take time to review this statement carefully. Check that the property values and tax estimates seem reasonable for your area. If something looks off, don't hesitate to contact your servicer or speak with a loan professional like Tyler Henschel at CMG Home Loans, who can help explain the details.

Can You Do Anything About an Escrow Increase?

While you can't always prevent escrow increases (especially when taxes or insurance genuinely rise), here are a few options:

Request an Escrow Account Review

If you believe your servicer's estimates are too high, you can request a formal review. This is particularly worthwhile if your property tax assessment recently decreased or you switched to a lower-cost insurance provider.

Pay Taxes and Insurance Separately

Depending on your loan type and situation, you may be able to pay property taxes and homeowners insurance directly instead of through escrow. This removes the escrow account from your mortgage payment entirely. However, your lender may require escrow if you have a lower down payment or certain loan programs.

Shop for Better Insurance Rates

While you can't control property taxes, you can reduce escrow increases related to insurance by getting quotes from multiple providers. Bundling homeowners insurance with auto insurance or improving your home's safety features may lower your premiums.

Escrow Adjustments in the Texas Market

Texas homeowners face unique escrow pressures. Growing communities in the Houston and Katy areas often experience rising property valuations, which drive higher tax assessments. Additionally, the competitive Texas insurance market has seen premium adjustments as carriers respond to claims experience and inflation.

These aren't reasons to panic — they're simply part of homeownership in a dynamic state. Understanding what's driving your escrow increase helps you plan your budget and make informed decisions about your loan.

Moving Forward with Confidence

An escrow increase is frustrating, but it's not unusual, and you're not powerless. By understanding the reasons behind the adjustment, reviewing your escrow analysis statement, and exploring your options, you can take control of your mortgage costs.
