Why Did Underwriting Ask for More Documents? A Guide for Houston and Katy Homebuyers

If you're in the middle of the mortgage application process in the Houston or Katy area, receiving a request for additional documents from underwriting can feel frustrating and confusing. You've already submitted what you thought was everything needed—so why do they want more? The truth is, document requests during underwriting are completely normal and actually a standard part of protecting both you and the lender. Understanding why this happens can help ease your concerns and keep your loan process moving forward smoothly.

Tyler Henschel, Senior Loan Officer at CMG Home Loans in Katy, TX, has guided thousands of borrowers through this exact situation. According to Tyler, "Document requests are a sign that underwriting is doing their job thoroughly. It's not a red flag—it's part of the verification process that ensures everything is accurate and compliant with lending guidelines."

The Role of the Underwriting Process

Underwriting is the critical stage in your mortgage application where a trained professional reviews your entire financial profile, the property details, and the loan structure to determine whether the loan meets investor and regulatory guidelines. This isn't just paperwork—it's a protective measure that verifies your ability to repay and ensures the property is a sound investment for the lender.

During underwriting, the underwriter's job is to verify every claim you've made on your application. They're looking at your income, assets, employment history, credit, and much more. When something doesn't quite line up or needs clarification, they'll issue what's called a "conditional approval"—which means they'll approve your loan as long as you provide specific documentation to verify the details.

Common Reasons for Document Requests

Income Verification

One of the most frequent reasons underwriters request additional documents is to verify your income. Lenders need to confirm that you have a stable, reliable income source to support your new mortgage payment. This typically involves:

  • Recent pay stubs (usually the last 30 days)
  • W-2 forms from the past two years
  • Tax returns for self-employed borrowers or those with additional income sources
  • Offer letters if you've recently changed jobs
  • Bonus or commission documentation if these are part of your income calculation

If you're self-employed or have rental income, expect underwriting to dig deeper into your financial documentation. They may request profit and loss statements, business tax returns, or lease agreements to verify the legitimacy and consistency of that income.

Asset and Down Payment Documentation

Underwriters verify that your down payment funds come from legitimate sources. They're required to trace the origin of large deposits in your bank accounts. Common document requests include:

  • Bank statements covering the last 60 days
  • Documentation explaining large deposits (gift letters if money came from family, investment account statements if it came from savings or investments)
  • Written explanations for any unusual account activity

This is especially important for borrowers in the Houston and Katy markets where cash offers and large down payments are becoming increasingly common. Your lender needs to ensure compliance with anti-money laundering regulations and lending guidelines.

Credit and Debt Verification

Underwriters may ask for documentation related to debts listed on your credit report, particularly if:

  • Recent accounts appear on your credit report that weren't disclosed on your application
  • There are outstanding collections, judgments, or late payments that need explanation
  • Existing debt balances on your credit report don't match what you reported
  • The underwriter needs verification of accounts in forbearance, deferment, or a payment plan

In these situations, you may be asked to provide written explanations, proof of payment, or documentation showing the current status of these accounts.

Employment History Clarification

If you've had job changes, gaps in employment, or are in a probationary period, underwriting will typically request documentation such as:

  • Offer letters for new employment
  • Written explanations for employment gaps
  • Documentation showing successful completion of probationary periods
  • Verification of employment (VOE) directly from your employer

Career transitions are common, especially in the greater Houston area where many industries experience regular workforce changes. Underwriters simply need to verify that your current employment is stable and ongoing.

Property and Appraisal Issues

Sometimes underwriters request documents related to the property itself:

  • Explanation letters regarding property condition issues noted in the appraisal
  • Repair estimates or proof of completed repairs
  • Title documents or surveys to clarify boundary or ownership questions
  • Homeowner association (HOA) documentation and financials

If the appraisal comes in lower than expected or identifies concerns, you may need to provide documentation addressing these issues.

How to Respond Effectively to Document Requests

When underwriting requests additional documents, here's how to handle it smoothly:

  • Respond quickly: The faster you provide documents, the faster your loan can move to closing. Aim to respond within 24-48 hours if possible.
  • Be thorough: Provide everything requested, not just part of it. Missing documents will trigger another request and delay your timeline.
  • Include written explanations: If underwriting asks for an explanation of something unusual (like a large deposit or employment gap), provide clear, honest written explanations.
  • Communicate with your loan officer: Your loan officer, like Tyler Henschel at CMG Home Loans, can help you understand what's being requested and guide you on the best way to respond.
  • Avoid making changes: Don't make large deposits, open new credit accounts, or make other financial changes while your loan is in underwriting, as this could trigger additional requests.

What Happens After You Submit Documents

Once you've submitted the requested documents, underwriting will review them and either:

  • Issue another set of conditional requests if clarification is still needed
  • Grant "clear to close" status, meaning your loan is approved and ready to move to the final closing steps
  • Deny the loan if the documents reveal information that doesn't meet lending guidelines

Most loans move to "clear to close" after the initial round of document requests. Additional rounds of requests are less common but may occur if new issues are discovered during the review process.

The Bottom Line

Document requests during underwriting aren't something to dread—they're simply part of the mortgage lending process. Whether you're a first-time homebuyer or an experienced real estate investor in the Katy or Houston market, underwriters will verify your information thoroughly. This protects everyone involved and ensures that loans are made responsibly.
