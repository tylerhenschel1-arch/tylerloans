Why Did My Mortgage Payment Go Up in Texas?

If you're a homeowner in the Katy or Houston area, you might have recently received a shock when opening your mortgage statement. Your monthly payment went up, and you're wondering why. You're not alone. Many Texas homeowners are experiencing unexpected increases in their mortgage payments, and understanding the reasons behind these changes is the first step toward managing your finances better.

At CMG Home Loans, we work with countless homeowners who have questions about their mortgage payments. Tyler Herschel, our Senior Loan Officer in Katy, TX, frequently helps clients understand the factors that can cause payment increases. Let's explore the most common reasons your mortgage payment might have gone up and what you can do about it.

Common Reasons Your Mortgage Payment Increased

Property Tax Increases

One of the most common culprits behind rising mortgage payments in Texas is an increase in property taxes. Texas is known for its relatively affordable cost of living, but property tax assessments can change annually. If your home's assessed value has increased, your property taxes will likely rise as well.

Your mortgage payment typically includes a portion that goes into an escrow account to cover property taxes. When your county or local tax assessor increases the assessed value of your home, your lender adjusts your monthly payment to ensure there's enough money in escrow to cover the higher tax bill when it comes due.

In the Houston and Katy areas, property values have appreciated significantly over the past few years, which naturally leads to higher property tax assessments. It's worth reviewing your property tax notice to confirm whether this is affecting your payment.

Homeowners Insurance Premium Changes

Another major factor in payment increases is homeowners insurance. Like property taxes, homeowners insurance is often bundled into your monthly mortgage payment through escrow. Insurance companies periodically reassess risk and adjust their premiums accordingly.

Several factors can trigger insurance premium increases:

  • Claims history on your property
  • Changes in the local insurance market
  • Age and condition of your home
  • Updates to your home's replacement cost value
  • Regional weather events or natural disasters

In Texas, where severe weather and occasional hurricanes can impact the insurance landscape, these increases are not uncommon. Your lender will adjust your escrow payment when your insurance premium changes to ensure adequate coverage.

HOA Fee Increases

If you live in a community with a homeowners association, HOA fees can increase annually. While HOA fees aren't technically part of your mortgage payment, they are a regular housing cost that affects your overall monthly housing expense. Many communities in the greater Houston and Katy areas have HOAs, and these organizations may raise fees to cover increased maintenance costs, repairs, or amenities.

Escrow Account Adjustments

Your mortgage lender conducts an annual escrow analysis to ensure your account has enough money to cover property taxes and insurance for the coming year. If the analysis shows a shortage—meaning there won't be enough money set aside—your lender will increase your monthly escrow payment to build up the account.

This is a routine part of homeownership, but it can feel like a sudden surprise if you're not expecting it. Your lender should provide documentation explaining the adjustment, which is helpful to review.

ARM Rate Adjustments (If You Have an Adjustable-Rate Mortgage)

If you have an adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM), your interest rate may have changed according to the terms of your loan. ARM loans typically have an initial fixed period, after which the rate can adjust periodically based on market conditions. If you're in the adjustment phase, your rate and payment could increase significantly.

This is one of the most impactful reasons for payment increases. If you're unsure whether you have an ARM or a fixed-rate mortgage, review your loan documents or contact your loan servicer for clarification.

What You Can Do About Rising Mortgage Payments

Review Your Loan Documents

Start by carefully reviewing your mortgage statement and any notices from your lender. These documents should explain what caused your payment increase. Understanding the breakdown between principal, interest, taxes, and insurance is essential.

Challenge Your Property Tax Assessment

If property taxes are the culprit, you may have the right to challenge your assessment. Texas allows homeowners to protest property tax assessments if they believe the valuation is incorrect. The process typically involves filing a formal protest with your local appraisal district. Many Houston and Katy homeowners have successfully reduced their tax burden through this process.

Shop for Homeowners Insurance

Insurance premiums can vary significantly between providers. If your insurance cost has increased, get quotes from several insurers to see if you can find more competitive rates. Even a small savings on your monthly premium adds up over time.

Consider Your Refinancing Options

If your payment increase is due to an ARM rate adjustment or if you simply want to explore your options, refinancing might be worth considering.
