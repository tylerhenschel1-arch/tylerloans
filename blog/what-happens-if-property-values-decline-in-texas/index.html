What happens if property values decline in Texas

By Tyler Henschel · June 03, 2026 · Katy, TX

What Happens If Property Values Decline in Texas?

The Texas real estate market has historically been one of the most resilient in the nation, with strong growth in major metros like Houston and Katy. However, like any market, property values can fluctuate due to economic conditions, interest rate changes, local development shifts, or broader financial pressures. If you're a homeowner in Texas, it's important to understand what happens when property values decline and how it might affect your financial position.

As someone who works with homeowners every day, Tyler Herschel, Senior Loan Officer at CMG Home Loans in Katy, TX, knows firsthand the concerns that arise when home values dip. In this guide, we'll explore the real implications of declining property values and what options may be available to you.

Understanding Equity and Home Value Decline

When you purchase a home, your equity is the difference between what your home is worth and what you owe on your mortgage. If your home is worth $350,000 and you owe $280,000, you have $70,000 in equity. When property values decline, that equity cushion shrinks—or in severe cases, disappears entirely.

What Is Negative Equity?

Negative equity occurs when your mortgage balance exceeds your home's current market value. For example, if your home's value drops to $250,000 but you still owe $280,000, you're underwater on your loan. This situation, sometimes called being "upside down," can create real challenges if you need to sell or refinance.

In the Houston and Katy markets, while dramatic declines are less common than in some regions, economic downturns or local market shifts can still impact property values. Understanding this risk is part of responsible homeownership.

How Declining Property Values Affect Your Mortgage

Your Monthly Payment Stays the Same

One important point: if you have a mortgage with a stable interest rate structure, your monthly mortgage payment won't change just because your home's value declines. You'll continue to pay the same principal and interest amount you agreed to at closing. The decline in value doesn't directly alter that obligation.

Property Taxes and Insurance May Adjust

However, there can be indirect effects. In Texas, property taxes are based on assessed value. If your home's assessed value drops significantly, your property tax bill may decrease over time—though reassessments don't happen instantly. Property insurance, on the other hand, is typically based on replacement cost rather than market value, so it may not change proportionally with home value declines.

Refinancing Becomes Harder

One of the most significant challenges with declining property values is refinancing. Lenders typically require a loan-to-value (LTV) ratio within certain limits. If your home's value has dropped, refinancing becomes more difficult or impossible without bringing cash to the table. This is where talking to an experienced loan officer like Tyler Henschel becomes valuable—he can help you understand whether refinancing is still an option and what alternatives might exist.

Selling Your Home When Values Decline

If you need to sell during a market downturn, you may face challenges:

  • Selling at a loss: You might receive less for your home than you originally paid, which can be emotionally and financially difficult.
  • Covering the shortfall: If you're underwater, you may need to bring cash to closing to pay off the difference between the sale price and what you owe.
  • Short sales: In some cases, lenders may agree to a short sale, where the home sells for less than the outstanding mortgage balance. This is a complex process with significant implications for your credit and finances.

In the Katy and Houston areas, where the market has generally been strong, outright negative equity situations are less common than they were nationally during the 2008 housing crisis. However, it's still important to be aware of these possibilities.

Steps to Protect Yourself From Property Value Decline

Buy Below Market Value When Possible

Starting with equity from day one gives you a buffer. A larger down payment means more built-in equity that protects you if values dip.

Maintain and Improve Your Home

Regular maintenance and strategic upgrades help maintain your property's appeal and value. Well-maintained homes are more resilient during market downturns.

Stay Informed About Your Market

Keep track of local real estate trends in your neighborhood. Understanding what's happening in the broader Katy and Houston markets can help you make informed decisions about your home and finances.

Review Your Mortgage Strategy

Having a solid mortgage structure in place is foundational. If you're considering a purchase or refinance, working with a knowledgeable loan officer ensures you're in the best position if market conditions shift. What If You Already Have Declining Equity?

If you're already concerned about your home's value relative to your mortgage balance, you have several options to explore:

  • Loan modification: Some lenders offer programs to modify loan terms if you're struggling.
  • Refinancing (if you have sufficient equity): Even modest equity can allow refinancing under certain circumstances, though approval depends on many factors including credit score and income verification.
  • Home improvement investment: Strategic upgrades can help stabilize or increase value over time.
  • Long-term holding: Real estate historically recovers over extended periods. If you can afford to stay in your home, patience often pays off.

The Bottom Line

Property value decline is a real concern for homeowners, but it doesn't have to derail your financial plans if you're informed and proactive. In strong markets like Katy and Houston, dramatic downturns are less likely, but being prepared is always wise. Understanding how decline affects your equity, your refinancing options, and your long-term plans helps you make confident decisions about your home and mortgage.
