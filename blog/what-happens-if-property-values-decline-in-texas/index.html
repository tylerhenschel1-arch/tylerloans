What happens if property values decline in Texas

By Tyler Henschel · June 03, 2026 · Katy, TX

What Happens If Property Values Decline in Texas?

If you're a homeowner in Katy, Houston, or anywhere across Texas, you've likely benefited from years of steady property value growth. But what happens when the market shifts and property values start to decline? It's a concern many Texas homeowners are asking about, especially as they think about their long-term financial security and home equity position. Understanding how declining property values affect you—and what options might be available—is essential for making informed decisions about your mortgage and financial future.

How Declining Property Values Impact Your Home Equity

Your home equity is the difference between what your home is worth and what you still owe on your mortgage. When property values decline in the Houston and Katy markets, your home's market value drops, which directly reduces your equity position. For example, if your home was worth $350,000 and you owe $250,000 on your mortgage, you have $100,000 in equity. If property values decline and your home is now worth $320,000, your equity shrinks to $70,000—even though you've made the same mortgage payments.

This reduction in equity can have ripple effects across your financial life. It may limit your ability to borrow against your home, refinance your mortgage, or tap into your equity for major expenses like home repairs, education, or other investments.

The Risk of Being Underwater on Your Mortgage

In more severe market downturns, property values can decline enough that homeowners end up owing more on their mortgage than their home is worth. This situation is called being "underwater" or having negative equity. While Texas markets have historically recovered from downturns, it's important to understand the implications if this were to happen:

  • Refinancing becomes difficult or impossible without a substantial down payment
  • Selling your home may require you to bring cash to the closing table
  • Your options for accessing home equity financing become severely limited
  • Moving for a job or personal reasons becomes more complicated financially

The good news is that the Katy and Houston real estate markets have demonstrated resilience over the long term. However, it's wise to understand these risks and plan accordingly.

How Property Value Declines Affect Homeowners Insurance and Taxes

While declining property values reduce your equity, they can actually have a positive effect on your property taxes in Texas. Property taxes are often based on assessed value, so if your home's assessed value decreases, your property tax bill may go down as well. This could free up cash flow in your monthly budget.

However, homeowners insurance is typically based on the replacement cost of your home, not its market value. So declining property values won't significantly reduce your insurance premiums. It's still important to review your coverage regularly to ensure you're adequately protected.

What You Can Do If Property Values Are Declining

If you're concerned about declining property values in your area, there are several proactive steps you can consider:

  • Focus on your mortgage payments: Continuing to make regular, on-time payments builds equity regardless of market conditions. This is the most fundamental way to strengthen your financial position.
  • Avoid overextending your finances: If you have equity in your home, avoid taking on too much additional debt against it. This can leave you vulnerable if property values decline further.
  • Invest in your home: Strategic upgrades and maintenance can help preserve your property's value and appeal to future buyers. Focus on improvements that offer good return on investment.
  • Review your mortgage terms: If you have an adjustable-rate mortgage or are concerned about rate changes, speaking with a loan officer about your options might provide peace of mind.
  • Think long-term: Real estate markets are cyclical. If you're not planning to sell in the next few years, short-term value declines may matter less than your long-term equity building through mortgage payments.

The Texas Real Estate Market: Historical Perspective

While short-term declines can happen, Texas—especially the Houston and Katy regions—has a strong history of property value recovery. Population growth, business expansion, and economic development in the greater Houston area have historically supported real estate values over the long term. This doesn't guarantee future performance, but it does provide context for understanding market cycles.

That said, local conditions matter. Some neighborhoods and communities may experience different value trajectories than others. Understanding your specific area's market fundamentals can help you make better decisions about your home and mortgage.
