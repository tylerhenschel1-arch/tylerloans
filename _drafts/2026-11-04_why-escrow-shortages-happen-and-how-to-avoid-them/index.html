Why escrow shortages happen and how to avoid them

By Tyler Henschel · November 04, 2026 · Houston, TX

Understanding Escrow Shortages: A Homeowner's Guide

If you've received a letter from your lender about an escrow shortage, you're not alone. Thousands of homeowners across the Houston metro—from Tomball and Cypress to Sugar Land and Pearland—face this issue every year. It can feel confusing and stressful, but understanding what causes escrow shortages and knowing how to prevent them puts you back in control of your finances.

An escrow shortage occurs when the money you've set aside through your monthly mortgage payments isn't enough to cover your property taxes, homeowners insurance, and other escrowed expenses when they come due. Rather than a surprise bill, your lender typically allows you to pay the shortage over time, but it increases your monthly payment. The good news? Most escrow shortages are preventable with the right knowledge and planning.

What Is an Escrow Account, and Why Does It Matter?

When you take out a mortgage, your lender often requires you to maintain an escrow account—a neutral holding account managed by your lender or a third party. Each month, a portion of your mortgage payment goes into this account to cover:

  • Property taxes
  • Homeowners insurance premiums
  • HOA fees (if applicable)
  • Mortgage insurance (if applicable)

Your lender estimates how much you'll need to set aside annually, divides that by 12, and adds it to your monthly payment. This system ensures these critical expenses are paid on time and protects the lender's interest in the property.

Why Do Escrow Shortages Happen?

Rising Property Tax Assessments

The most common culprit behind escrow shortages is an increase in property taxes. Whether you own a home in Memorial, Magnolia, or Bellaire, local tax assessments can climb year after year. When your county reassesses your property's value, your tax bill rises—but your monthly escrow payment was calculated based on the previous year's amount. That gap creates a shortage.

Increased Insurance Premiums

Insurance costs don't stay static. A windstorm event, claims in your area, or simply market rate increases can drive up your homeowners insurance premium. If your insurance company raises rates mid-year or between renewal periods, your escrow account may not have enough to cover the new bill when it comes due.

Inaccurate Initial Escrow Estimates

At closing, your lender estimates escrow costs based on available data. However, if that estimate was too low—perhaps because recent tax increases weren't fully reflected or insurance premiums were underestimated—you'll face a shortage when actual bills arrive.

Changes in Your Loan or Property Status

Certain life changes trigger escrow recalculations. For example, if you paid off mortgage insurance (PMI) or made a large principal payment, your escrow account may not have been properly adjusted. Similarly, changes to your insurance coverage or property tax exemptions can affect the amount needed.

Lender Servicing Errors

Though less common, mistakes in how a lender calculates or manages your escrow account can lead to shortages. This is why it's important to review your annual escrow statement carefully.

How to Avoid Escrow Shortages

Monitor Your Escrow Statement Annually

Your lender is required to send you an annual escrow statement. Don't ignore it—review it carefully. Check that the property taxes and insurance amounts listed match your actual bills. If there's a significant difference, contact your lender immediately to discuss a correction.

Stay Informed About Property Tax Changes

In Texas, property tax assessments happen annually. Homeowners across the Houston area, including those in Spring, Conroe, and Friendswood, should review their tax assessment notices when they arrive. If you believe your assessment is too high, you have the right to protest it. Even a modest reduction in assessed value can lower your tax bill and help prevent future escrow shortages.

Shop Your Homeowners Insurance Periodically

Insurance premiums can vary significantly between companies. By comparing quotes every two to three years, you may find a lower rate that reduces your escrow burden. Even a $30 monthly savings on insurance adds up to $360 per year—money that could prevent a shortage.

Maintain an Open Line of Communication with Your Lender

If you know your property taxes or insurance premiums are increasing, reach out to your lender proactively. They can recalculate your escrow account and adjust your payment before a shortage occurs, spreading the adjustment across several months rather than hitting you with a lump-sum bill.

Build a Small Reserve

While your lender caps how much can be held in escrow, setting aside an additional small amount each month in a dedicated savings account can serve as a buffer against unexpected increases. This safety net means you're prepared if a shortage does occur.

Consider Your Payment Options

If your lender notifies you of a shortage, ask about payment options. Many lenders allow you to pay it in installments rather than in full, spreading the burden across several months.

What Should You Do If You Receive an Escrow Shortage Notice?

First, take a deep breath. An escrow shortage isn't a crisis, and it doesn't mean you've done anything wrong. Here's what to do:

  • Read the notice carefully. Understand the exact amount and the reason for the shortage.
  • Verify the numbers. Cross-check the property taxes and insurance amounts against your actual bills.
  • Contact your lender. Ask about your payment options and request an explanation if anything seems unclear.
  • Consider refinancing. Depending on current market conditions and your loan terms, refinancing might make sense to reset your escrow account and potentially improve your overall loan terms.
