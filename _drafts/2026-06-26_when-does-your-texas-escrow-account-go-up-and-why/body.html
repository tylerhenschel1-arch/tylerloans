What Is an Escrow Account?

Your escrow account is a holding account managed by your mortgage lender or loan servicer. Each month, a portion of your mortgage payment goes into this account. The servicer then uses those funds to pay certain expenses on your behalf, including:

  • Property taxes
  • Homeowners insurance premiums
  • Mortgage insurance (if applicable)
  • HOA fees (in communities with mandatory HOAs)

Think of it as a convenient way to ensure these critical bills get paid on time. Rather than tracking multiple due dates yourself, your lender handles it as part of your monthly mortgage payment.

Why Does Your Escrow Account Balance Increase?

Your escrow account balance goes up for one primary reason: the costs it covers are rising. Let's explore the main drivers.

Property Tax Increases

Property taxes are the single largest factor affecting escrow increases across Texas. Every few years, your local county assessor revalues residential properties. When your home's assessed value goes up—which is common in growing areas like Cypress, Pearland, and the Memorial area—your property tax bill increases proportionally.

Texas is a property-tax-heavy state, meaning residents rely less on income tax and more on property taxes to fund schools, infrastructure, and local services. As the Houston region continues to grow and home values appreciate, many homeowners see their property tax assessments climb. This directly flows into your escrow account calculation.

Homeowners Insurance Premium Hikes

Homeowners insurance costs have been rising across Texas in recent years. Weather-related claims, inflation, and increased replacement costs all drive insurance premiums higher. When your insurance company renews your policy at a higher rate, that increase gets reflected in your escrow analysis.

Whether you're in Spring, Bellaire, Heights, or Friendswood, you're subject to the same insurance market pressures. Older homes may face steeper increases due to higher replacement costs and potential claim history.

Mortgage Insurance (PMI) Adjustments

If you put down less than 20% on your conventional mortgage, you're likely paying private mortgage insurance. Your PMI amount can adjust based on your loan balance and other factors. While PMI typically decreases over time as you build equity, it still factors into your escrow account.

HOA Fee Increases

Many newer developments across the Houston metro—from Magnolia to Conroe to The Woodlands—have homeowners associations. HOA fees often increase annually to cover rising maintenance, insurance, and reserve fund contributions. If your HOA fees go up, your escrow payment rises accordingly.

How Lenders Calculate Escrow Increases

Mortgage servicers conduct an annual escrow analysis (sometimes twice a year) to determine whether your current escrow payment covers the upcoming year's costs. Here's what typically happens:

  • Current Year Review: The servicer compares actual payments made from your escrow account against what was estimated. If property taxes came in higher than expected, there's a shortfall.
  • Next Year Projection: Based on tax assessments, insurance quotes, and other known costs, they forecast your upcoming expenses.
  • New Payment Calculation: They divide the total estimated annual escrow costs by 12 months. If costs are up 10%, your monthly escrow payment goes up roughly 10%.
  • Shortage or Surplus: If there's a shortfall from the prior year, lenders may require you to pay it back over 12 months, further increasing your payment.

What You Can Do About Escrow Increases

While you can't control property tax rates or insurance markets, you do have some options:

Review Your Property Tax Assessment

If you believe your assessed value is too high, you can file a property tax protest with your local appraisal district. Many homeowners across the greater Houston area successfully challenge assessments and secure lower valuations, which then reduces their escrow payments.

Shop for Homeowners Insurance

Insurance rates vary significantly between carriers. Getting competing quotes every year or two could reveal a better rate elsewhere. Even a modest savings on insurance lowers your escrow payment.

Understand Your Escrow Analysis

Review the escrow statement your servicer sends each year. Make sure the numbers make sense and that no errors inflated the calculation. If you spot a discrepancy, contact your servicer immediately.

Ask About Escrow Waiver Options

Depending on your loan product and equity position, some borrowers can request to handle property taxes and insurance independently rather than through escrow. This gives you more control—though it also means you're responsible for paying these bills on time.
