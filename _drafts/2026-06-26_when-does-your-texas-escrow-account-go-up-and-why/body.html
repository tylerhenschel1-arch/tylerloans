Why Your Texas Escrow Account Balance Goes Up (And What It Means for Your Mortgage Payment)

If you're a homeowner in the greater Houston area—whether you're in Cypress, Sugar Land, Pearland, or any of the surrounding communities—you've probably noticed something unexpected on your mortgage statement: your escrow account balance increased, and your monthly payment went up along with it.

This catches many homeowners off guard. You're making the same payment you've always made, so why is your bill suddenly higher? The answer lies in understanding how escrow accounts work and what triggers adjustments throughout the year.

Let me walk you through the mechanics of escrow, when balances rise, and what you can do about it.

What Is an Escrow Account?

An escrow account is a separate account managed by your mortgage servicer that holds funds for expenses related to your home. These typically include:

  • Property taxes
  • Homeowners insurance premiums
  • HOA fees (if applicable)
  • Mortgage insurance (PMI, FHA MIP, or similar)

Instead of paying these costs directly and on different schedules, your lender collects a portion of each expense with your regular monthly mortgage payment. When bills come due, the servicer pays them from your escrow account on your behalf.

This system protects both you and the lender. For the lender, it ensures that property taxes and insurance stay current—critical protections for their investment in your home. For you, it simplifies budgeting by rolling everything into one predictable monthly payment.

When and Why Does Your Escrow Account Balance Increase?

Escrow accounts go up for one primary reason: the cost of the expenses they cover has risen. This happens most often when property taxes increase or insurance premiums go up.

Property Tax Increases

In Texas, property taxes are typically reassessed annually. Your home's assessed value may increase based on market conditions, county appraisals, or changes to your property. When the assessed value rises, so does your annual tax bill.

For example, if your property taxes increased by $600 annually, your escrow account must accumulate an extra $50 per month (divided across 12 months). That increase gets added to your regular mortgage payment.

Homeowners Insurance Premium Increases

Insurance companies regularly adjust premiums based on claims history, inflation, and risk factors. If your insurer raises your annual premium by $300, your escrow payment increases by approximately $25 per month.

In Houston and surrounding areas like Tomball, The Woodlands, and Magnolia, insurance costs can fluctuate due to local weather patterns, storm history, and market conditions.

Mortgage Insurance Adjustments

If you have PMI (private mortgage insurance) on a conventional loan or FHA MIP (mortgage insurance premium) on an FHA loan, changes to these payments also affect your escrow account. Your servicer will recalculate these annually based on your current loan balance and loan type.

HOA Fees

Homeowners associations in neighborhoods throughout Bellaire, Memorial, Heights, and other Houston communities may increase their annual fees. These assessments get reflected in your escrow account and ultimately your monthly payment.

The Annual Escrow Analysis

Once a year, your mortgage servicer performs an escrow analysis. This is a detailed review that projects the costs for the upcoming year and determines whether your current escrow payment is sufficient.

During this analysis, the servicer looks at:

  • Actual taxes and insurance paid during the previous year
  • Projected costs for the upcoming 12 months
  • Your current escrow account balance
  • Cushion requirements (usually 2 months of expenses)

If projected costs are higher than what you're currently paying into escrow, the servicer increases your monthly payment. If costs have decreased, they may lower your payment (though this is less common).

Understanding the Escrow Cushion

Lenders are required by federal regulation to maintain a "cushion" in your escrow account—typically two months' worth of anticipated escrow expenses. This buffer protects against timing mismatches when bills arrive unexpectedly or costs are higher than projected.

This cushion requirement means your escrow account often holds extra funds beyond what's immediately needed. During the annual analysis, if your cushion grows too large (beyond the permitted amount), you may receive a refund. Conversely, if the cushion is deficient, your payment will increase to rebuild it.

What Homeowners Should Know

Escrow Increases Are Normal

Rising escrow payments aren't unusual or necessarily a sign of a problem. They reflect real increases in the costs of home ownership. In the Houston metro area, property tax increases have been a consistent trend, particularly in fast-growing areas like Conroe, Spring, Friendswood, and other suburbs.

You Have Options

While you can't prevent tax and insurance increases, you can take some proactive steps:

  • Review your property tax assessment. If you believe your home's assessed value is too high, you can file a protest with your local appraisal district in Harris County, Montgomery County, Fort Bend County, or whichever jurisdiction you're in.
  • Shop for insurance. Homeowners insurance is competitive. Getting quotes from multiple carriers annually can help you find better rates.
  • Ask about your escrow statement. Your servicer must provide you with an annual escrow account statement. Review it carefully to understand exactly where increases are coming from.

Distinguish Between Escrow and Principal-and-Interest Changes

It's important to note that escrow changes are separate from changes to your principal-and-interest payment. Your P&I stays the same on a fixed-rate loan (the most common mortgage type). What changes is the escrow component—the portion of your payment that goes into the account for taxes, insurance, and other expenses.

What Should You Do If Your Payment Increases?

First, don't panic. Request and review your escrow account statement from your servicer. Look for:

  • How much property taxes increased
  • How much insurance premiums changed
  • Whether HOA fees or other expenses rose
  • The new escrow cushion balance

If the increase seems unusually large or you don't understand the breakdown, contact your servicer and ask for clarification. You have the right to understand how your payment is calculated.
