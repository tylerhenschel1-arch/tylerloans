What Inspectors Look for in New Construction Homes in Houston

Buying a new construction home in the Greater Houston area—whether you're looking in Sugar Land, Cypress, The Woodlands, or anywhere across the metro—is an exciting milestone. But before you close on that brand-new build, a thorough home inspection is one of the most important steps you can take. Many first-time buyers are surprised to learn that new doesn't automatically mean perfect. Even homes fresh from the builder can have defects, missed details, or construction issues that an experienced inspector will catch.

As a Senior Loan Officer at CMG Home Loans serving the Houston metro, Tyler Henschel works with new construction buyers every day. He often recommends that his clients prioritize a detailed inspection before finalizing their purchase. "A good inspector can save you thousands down the road," Tyler says. "Even new homes have issues—and knowing about them upfront changes the negotiation and gives you peace of mind."

Let's walk through what professional home inspectors are looking for when they evaluate new construction properties across the Houston area.

Structural and Foundation Issues

The foundation is literally where everything else sits. Inspectors will examine the foundation for signs of settling, cracks, or water intrusion. In the Houston area, where clay soil and humidity are common challenges, foundation concerns are taken seriously.

  • Hairline cracks (very minor) versus structural cracks (wider or longer)
  • Evidence of water seepage or moisture in the crawlspace or basement
  • Uneven settling or sloping floors
  • Proper grading around the perimeter to direct water away from the home

Inspectors will also verify that the builder followed proper construction standards and that any required foundation warranty paperwork is in place.

Roof Condition and Attic Ventilation

Your roof is your first line of defense against Houston's intense sun, humidity, and occasional severe storms. A new roof should be in excellent condition, but inspectors look for:

  • Proper shingle installation with no lifted, cracked, or missing shingles
  • Correct flashing around chimneys, vents, and skylights
  • Adequate attic ventilation to prevent moisture buildup and premature deterioration
  • Proper roof decking without soft spots or gaps
  • Compliance with local wind and weather codes (important in Houston)

A thorough attic inspection is especially valuable in new construction. Inspectors will check insulation levels, ensure proper air sealing, and confirm that ventilation pathways are clear.

Electrical Systems

New homes should have modern electrical systems that meet current code. Inspectors verify:

  • Proper grounding and bonding throughout the home
  • Correct breaker sizing and labeling on the electrical panel
  • Adequate outlet placement and functioning receptacles
  • Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter (GFCI) outlets in kitchens, bathrooms, and wet areas
  • Proper installation of light fixtures and switches
  • No reversed polarity or other electrical hazards

Even in brand-new homes, wiring mistakes or incomplete work can happen during construction. A professional inspection catches these before they become safety or functionality problems.

Plumbing and Water Systems

Water issues can lead to expensive repairs and mold problems, making plumbing inspection critical. Inspectors look at:

  • Proper water pressure and flow from all fixtures
  • No leaks under sinks, around toilets, or in visible supply lines
  • Correct slope and support for drain lines
  • Proper venting of drain pipes
  • Hot water heater installation and operation
  • Water line material (copper, PEX, etc.) installed per code

In Pearland, Tomball, and other Houston-area neighborhoods, inspectors also check for proper backflow prevention and municipal water connection compliance.

HVAC System Functionality

Houston summers are no joke. Your HVAC system needs to work properly from day one. Inspectors will:

  • Test heating and cooling operation in all zones
  • Verify proper thermostat function and calibration
  • Check ductwork for proper installation, sealing, and insulation
  • Confirm adequate refrigerant charge and system efficiency
  • Review maintenance access and filter locations
  • Verify proper combustion air supply (for gas furnaces)

An undersized or incorrectly installed system can mean high energy bills and uncomfortable living conditions year-round.

Windows, Doors, and Weather Sealing

Houston's humidity means that proper weather sealing is essential. Inspectors check:

  • Caulking and weatherstripping condition around all windows and exterior doors
  • Proper installation of windows—no gaps, rattling, or misalignment
  • Door operation and proper closing/latching
  • Sealed transitions where different materials meet

Any gaps or poor sealing can lead to moisture intrusion, mold, and energy inefficiency—all expensive problems to fix after closing.

Interior Finishes and Workmanship

Beyond the big-ticket systems, inspectors also examine the quality of everyday finishes:

  • Drywall condition—no large cracks, holes, or water damage
  • Paint coverage and quality
  • Flooring installation and finish (no gaps, buckling, or damage)
  • Cabinet and countertop alignment and function
  • Proper installation of appliances
  • Door and window operation throughout the home

These items might seem minor, but they affect your daily living experience and can indicate larger construction quality issues.

Building Code and Safety Compliance

Inspectors verify that the home was built to current Houston area building codes, including:

  • Proper stair construction and railings
  • Smoke and carbon monoxide detector installation
  • Egress windows in bedrooms
  • Proper handrails and guardrails
  • Fire-blocking and draft-stopping in walls

Tyler Henschel emphasizes that code compliance isn't just about passing an inspection—it's about your family's safety and the home's long-term value. "Builders should be meeting or exceeding code, not just skating by," he notes.

Water Intrusion and Moisture Issues

Given Houston's climate, moisture management is critical. Inspectors look for signs of:

  • Water stains on ceilings or walls
  • Mold or mildew odors
  • Proper grading and drainage around the foundation
  • Functional gutters and downspouts that extend properly away from the home
  • Vapor barriers in crawlspaces or basements

Even small moisture issues can grow into major mold problems if left unchecked in Houston's humid environment.
