Why Your Houston Mortgage Payment Changed This Year

If you're a homeowner anywhere across the Greater Houston area—whether you're in Sugar Land, Tomball, The Woodlands, or Cypress—you might have noticed something unexpected in your mortgage statement recently. Your monthly payment went up, and you're wondering why. You didn't refinance. You didn't take out a new loan. Yet somehow, your bill is higher than it was last year.

You're not alone. Thousands of Houston-area homeowners have experienced payment increases in 2024, and the reasons are often misunderstood. The good news? Once you understand what's happening, you can take control of your finances and plan accordingly.

The Most Common Culprit: Property Tax Increases

In Texas, property values have climbed steadily over the past few years, and Houston's booming real estate market means your home's assessed value likely increased. When your property's appraised value goes up, so do your property taxes—and that directly impacts your mortgage payment.

Here's how it works: Most mortgage servicers collect property taxes as part of your monthly escrow payment. Your servicer sets aside a portion of each payment into an escrow account, and at tax time, they pay your Harris County (or surrounding county) tax bill on your behalf. When the county reassesses your home's value and your tax liability increases, your servicer adjusts your monthly escrow contribution upward to ensure they'll have enough to cover next year's bill.

For homeowners in areas like Memorial, Bellaire, or Pearland—where property appreciation has been especially strong—this can mean meaningful increases to monthly payments. A $30,000 increase in home value could translate to several hundred dollars more in annual property taxes, which breaks down to $25–$50+ per month in added escrow.

Homeowners Insurance Premiums Are Rising Across Texas

Just as property tax escrow adjusts, so does homeowners insurance. Your mortgage servicer collects insurance premiums as part of your monthly payment and pays your insurance company directly. When insurance rates go up—and they have in Texas—your monthly payment increases.

Several factors are driving insurance costs higher:

  • Weather and natural disasters: Texas has experienced significant weather events in recent years, which has led insurers to adjust their rates upward.
  • Construction and material costs: The price to rebuild a home has increased, so insurers have raised premiums to reflect that risk.
  • Labor shortages: Claims adjusters and contractors are more expensive to hire, costs that get passed along to consumers.
  • Carrier exits: Some insurance companies have left the Texas market entirely, consolidating customers among remaining carriers and creating upward rate pressure.

If you received a notice that your insurance premium increased, ask your agent whether you can shop for a better rate elsewhere. Some homeowners in Friendswood, Spring, or Conroe have found savings by switching carriers, though you'll want to compare coverage carefully.

HOA Fee Increases and Special Assessments

If you live in a community with a homeowners association—common in subdivisions throughout Katy, The Woodlands, and Magnolia—your HOA fees might have increased as well. HOA boards regularly adjust dues to cover rising maintenance costs, reserve fund contributions, or special projects.

These increases aren't directly controlled by your mortgage servicer, but they're part of your total housing costs. Some servicers do collect HOA fees into escrow alongside taxes and insurance, so an HOA fee increase would show up in your mortgage payment.

Special assessments—one-time charges for major repairs or improvements—can be even more surprising. Before buying or refinancing, it's worth asking your realtor or HOA about any planned assessments.

Escrow Account Shortfalls and Adjustments

Sometimes mortgage servicers miscalculate how much to set aside in escrow. If taxes or insurance came in higher than expected, your servicer might adjust your monthly payment to catch up and ensure the account stays fully funded going forward.

By law, servicers must review your escrow account annually and notify you of any changes. You'll typically see this in a formal "Escrow Account Disclosure" statement. If you believe the adjustment is incorrect, you have the right to dispute it—contact your servicer's customer service department with documentation of your tax and insurance bills.

What You Can Do About Payment Increases

Review your escrow statement. Your servicer is required to send you an annual escrow account statement. Review it carefully to understand exactly what changed and why.

Shop for insurance. If insurance premiums are the main driver, contact a few insurance agents in your area (whether you're in Pearland, Heights, or Bellaire) to see if you can find competitive rates elsewhere. Don't sacrifice coverage for price, but don't overpay either.

Check your property tax assessment. If you believe your county's appraisal is too high, many Texas counties allow you to file a protest. The process is typically straightforward and free. Contact your local county appraisal district for details.

Consider your refinancing options. If your payment has increased significantly and you're interested in exploring a refinance, a mortgage professional can review your current loan and discuss whether it makes sense for your situation. Looking Ahead: Stay Informed

Payment increases are frustrating, but they're usually temporary or gradual. Understanding why they happen empowers you to budget more effectively and make informed financial decisions. Keep your escrow statements, tax notices, and insurance documents together so you have a clear picture of your housing costs.
