What Are Short-Term Rental Loans?

Short-term rental loans are specifically designed to finance properties that generate income through nightly or weekly rentals rather than traditional long-term leases. These loans evaluate your ability to repay based on rental income rather than your W-2 employment or business tax returns alone.

The key metric lenders use is called the Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR). In simple terms, DSCR measures the monthly gross rental income generated by your property and compares it to your total monthly housing payments (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and HOA fees, if applicable).

For short-term rental properties, many lenders require a minimum DSCR of around 1.25. This means your property's gross monthly rental income should be at least 1.25 times your monthly payment obligations. For example, if your monthly payment is $2,000, you'd need to generate approximately $2,500 in gross monthly rental income to meet the lender's requirements.

This approach makes sense: lenders want to see that your rental business can comfortably cover its debt and still generate a profit for you as the investor.

DSCR Loans vs. Traditional Mortgages

Traditional mortgage lenders focus heavily on your personal income, credit score, and employment history. They care less about the property's actual income potential. This works fine for primary residences or long-term rentals, but it creates friction for STR investors.

DSCR-based loans flip the script. Your personal income becomes secondary. What matters most is whether the property itself generates enough rental revenue to service the debt. This is especially valuable if you:

  • Are self-employed or have variable income
  • Recently started a new business or job
  • Have multiple investment properties and complex tax returns
  • Want to qualify based on the property's income rather than your personal finances

In many cases, you won't even need to provide personal tax returns—just rental income documentation like lease agreements, booking history, or property management statements.

The Role of Rental Income Documentation

When you apply for a short-term rental loan, lenders will want to see proof of your property's earning potential. For existing STR properties, this typically includes:

  • Booking platform records (Airbnb, VRBO, etc.) showing historical occupancy and nightly rates
  • Property management statements documenting actual revenue
  • Tax returns showing rental income reported to the IRS

For new STR properties you're planning to purchase, lenders may evaluate potential rental income based on comparable properties in the area, market reports, or a professional appraisal that factors in rental comps.

The more thorough and professional your documentation, the smoother your loan application will proceed. This is an area where working with an experienced loan officer—someone who understands both real estate investing and lending—makes all the difference.

Key Considerations for Galveston and Houston STR Investors

Seasonal Income Variability: Galveston properties especially can see dramatic swings between peak (spring break, summer, holidays) and off-season months. Lenders understand this and typically average income over a longer period to smooth out the fluctuations.

Property Type and Location: A beachfront condo in Galveston will have different lending dynamics than a three-bedroom home in Cypress. Lender appetite varies, and some specialize more in coastal properties while others focus on urban Houston neighborhoods like Heights or Friendsville.

Down Payment and Loan Limits: STR loans often require higher down payments (15–25%) compared to conventional mortgages, though this varies by lender and property profile. Loan amounts and terms also differ based on the lender's portfolio strategy.

Insurance and Property Management: Lenders will want to see that your property is properly insured for short-term rental use (standard homeowners policies often exclude or limit STR coverage). Professional property management is also strongly encouraged—some lenders prefer to see it, especially for loans with lower DSCR.
