How Bonuses, Commissions, and Overtime Income Are Calculated for Mortgages

When you're applying for a mortgage across the Greater Houston area—whether you're in Cypress, Sugar Land, The Woodlands, or anywhere in between—lenders want a complete picture of your financial situation. If a significant portion of your income comes from bonuses, commissions, or overtime, understanding how that income is evaluated during the mortgage process can make a real difference in your approval odds and loan terms.

Many borrowers don't realize that not all income is treated the same way by lenders. Your base salary might qualify easily, but variable income requires additional documentation and follows specific underwriting rules. Tyler Henschel, a Senior Loan Officer at CMG Home Loans serving the Houston metro, works with borrowers every day who earn money through non-traditional channels. "The key," he says, "is having the right documentation and knowing how lenders actually count that income."

Why Lenders Scrutinize Variable Income

Mortgage lenders are in the business of managing risk. Base salary is straightforward—it's usually guaranteed by an employment contract. Bonuses, commissions, and overtime, on the other hand, can fluctuate from month to month or year to year. That variability introduces uncertainty, which is why lenders apply stricter rules when evaluating how much of that income they can count toward your borrowing capacity.

The goal isn't to penalize you—it's to ensure that you can comfortably make your mortgage payments even if your variable income dips during an economic slowdown or if you change jobs.

Bonus Income Calculation Rules

Documentation Requirements

If you receive annual or periodic bonuses, lenders typically want to see:

  • Your most recent two years of tax returns (Form 1040 with Schedule C or W-2s)
  • A recent pay stub showing year-to-date bonus income
  • A letter from your employer on company letterhead confirming the bonus structure and amount
  • A signed employment contract or offer letter that details the bonus terms

The Two-Year Average Rule

Most lenders use a straightforward approach: they average your bonus income over the past two years. Here's how it works in practice:

  • Year 1 bonus: $8,000
  • Year 2 bonus: $10,000
  • Calculated bonus income: ($8,000 + $10,000) ÷ 2 = $9,000 annually

Only that $9,000 average is counted as qualifying income on your mortgage application. If your bonuses have been increasing or decreasing significantly, lenders may ask for additional context or recent correspondence from your employer.

When Bonuses Are Excluded

Some lenders may not count bonus income at all if:

  • You've been receiving bonuses for less than two years
  • There's a significant downward trend in bonus amounts over the past two years
  • Your employment contract doesn't guarantee bonuses going forward
  • Your employer letter doesn't explicitly confirm the bonus will continue

Commission Income Calculation

Self-Employed vs. Commission-Based W-2 Employee

Commission income is treated differently depending on whether you're a W-2 employee who earns commissions or a self-employed person who reports commission income on a business tax return.

For W-2 Employees Earning Commissions:

  • Lenders typically average your commission income over two years, similar to bonuses
  • Recent pay stubs showing commission year-to-date are essential
  • An employer letter confirming commission structure and likelihood of continuation is required
  • Your W-2 should reflect the commission component

For Self-Employed Commission-Based Income:

  • Lenders review two years of personal tax returns (Form 1040)
  • Business income from Schedule C (or Schedule C-EZ) is analyzed
  • The most recent year's income is often given more weight than prior years
  • Accountant-prepared or CPA-reviewed tax returns carry more credibility than self-prepared returns
  • Year-to-date profit and loss statements may be requested

The Commission Averaging Formula

Similar to bonuses, commission income is typically averaged:

  • Year 1 commission: $35,000
  • Year 2 commission: $42,000
  • Calculated commission income: ($35,000 + $42,000) ÷ 2 = $38,500 annually

Declining Commission Income

If your commission income has declined over the past two years, lenders may use only the most recent year's figure, or they might apply a discount factor. Some programs require a downward trending letter from your employer explaining the decline and confirming stability or recovery going forward.

Overtime Income Calculation

Documentation for Overtime

Overtime income can be a powerful way to increase your qualifying income, but it must be documented carefully:

  • Two years of personal tax returns showing overtime on your W-2
  • Recent pay stubs clearly showing overtime hours and pay
  • An employer letter on company letterhead confirming that overtime is a regular, continuing part of your job
  • Ideally, a history showing consistent overtime for at least two years

The Overtime Calculation Approach

Lenders average overtime income over two years, just like bonuses and commissions:

  • Year 1 overtime: $6,000
  • Year 2 overtime: $7,200
  • Calculated overtime income: ($6,000 + $7,200) ÷ 2 = $6,600 annually

This amount would then be added to your base W-2 income for debt-to-income ratio calculations.

When Overtime Doesn't Qualify

Lenders may exclude overtime income if:

  • You haven't worked overtime for at least two full years
  • Your employer cannot confirm that overtime will continue
  • Overtime hours have been declining significantly
  • Your industry or position is known for variable or temporary overtime (such as seasonal or project-based work)

Tips for Strengthening Your Variable Income Application

Stay Organized

Keep all documentation in one place: tax returns, recent pay stubs, employer letters, and employment contracts. Being organized speeds up the application process and shows you're serious about the application.

Get an Employer Letter Early

Don't wait until after you apply. Contact your HR or payroll department now and request a letter on company letterhead that confirms your variable income structure and its likelihood of continuation. Having this ready makes a strong impression on your lender.

Use Accountant-Prepared Returns
