What Closing Costs Should Buyers Expect in Texas

Buying a home in the Houston area is an exciting milestone, but it comes with more than just the down payment. Between the time you make an offer and the day you receive your keys, you'll encounter closing costs—a range of fees and charges that can add up quickly. Understanding what to expect helps you budget more effectively and avoid surprises at the closing table.

As a Senior Loan Officer at CMG Home Loans serving the Greater Houston metro, Tyler Henschel works with buyers throughout Sugar Land, The Woodlands, Pearland, Cypress, and beyond to help them navigate these expenses. Let's break down the typical closing costs you'll encounter as a Texas homebuyer.

What Are Closing Costs?

Closing costs are fees and expenses paid to finalize your mortgage and transfer ownership of the property. They typically range from 2% to 5% of your home's purchase price, though the exact amount varies based on the loan type, location, and lender. In Texas, you might pay closing costs of $4,000 to $10,000 or more on a $200,000 home, depending on several factors.

The good news? Some closing costs are negotiable, and in many cases, sellers can contribute to them. Understanding each line item puts you in a better position to negotiate and plan your finances.

Common Closing Costs in Texas

Loan Origination Fees

Your lender charges an origination fee to process and underwrite your loan. This typically runs 0.5% to 1% of the loan amount. While this fee isn't always negotiable, shopping with multiple lenders—like Tyler Henschel's team at CMG Home Loans—can help you find competitive terms.

Appraisal and Inspection Fees

Lenders require an appraisal to confirm the home's value, usually costing $400 to $600. A professional home inspection, while technically not a closing cost (you typically pay this before closing), often runs $300 to $500. Some buyers also order a termite inspection or radon test, adding another $100 to $300.

Title Search and Title Insurance

A title company searches property records to verify ownership and reveal any liens or claims. Title search fees usually cost $200 to $400. Title insurance protects you and your lender against ownership disputes and typically costs 0.5% to 1% of the purchase price. In Texas, the seller often pays for the owner's title insurance policy, but you may pay for the lender's policy.

Property Survey

If the home hasn't been surveyed recently, the lender may require one to verify property boundaries. A survey can range from $200 to $600, depending on the property's size and complexity.

Property Taxes and Insurance

At closing, you'll typically pay property taxes for any period between closing and the end of the tax year, plus prepaid homeowners insurance for the first few months. These amounts depend on your home's location and value. Property taxes vary significantly across the Houston area—for example, Spring and Memorial have different tax rates than Conroe or Friendswood.

HOA Fees and Transfer

If your home is in a homeowners association, you may pay HOA transfer fees, a portion of annual dues, and sometimes a resale certificate fee. These fees vary widely but can range from $100 to $500 or more.

Recording Fees

Texas counties charge recording fees to document the deed transfer. These are typically modest—usually under $100—but vary by county.

Underwriting and Processing Fees

Some lenders charge separate fees for underwriting and loan processing. These can range from $300 to $1,000 combined, depending on the lender's fee structure.

Credit Report and Verification Fees

Lenders pull your credit report and may charge a small fee ($25 to $75) along with fees for verifying employment and deposits, typically $25 to $50 each.

What About Discount Points or Origination Costs?

Some borrowers choose to buy discount points at closing to potentially lower their loan costs over time. This is entirely optional and depends on your financial situation and how long you plan to stay in the home.

Can You Reduce or Negotiate Closing Costs?

Absolutely. Here are several strategies:

  • Shop with multiple lenders: Different lenders charge different origination and processing fees. Getting quotes from multiple sources, including Tyler Henschel at CMG Home Loans, helps you compare.
  • Ask the seller to contribute: In a buyer's market, sellers sometimes cover closing costs to make a deal more attractive.
  • Look for lender credits: Some lenders offer closing cost credits in exchange for a slightly higher interest rate, depending on your situation.
  • Choose your title company: While many lenders recommend a title company, you often have the right to choose your own, which may have lower fees.
  • Verify all charges: Request a Loan Estimate early and review it carefully. Compare it with your Closing Disclosure three days before closing to catch any unexpected fees.

Texas-Specific Closing Cost Considerations

Texas has some unique aspects that affect closing costs:

  • No state income tax: While Texas doesn't have a state income tax, property taxes are relatively high, especially in fast-growing areas like Tomball and The Woodlands.
  • Seller responsibility for title insurance: In most Texas transactions, the seller pays for the owner's title insurance policy, which can save buyers several hundred dollars.
  • County recording fees vary: Harris County (where Houston, Memorial, and Bellaire are located) has different recording fees than Montgomery County (Spring, Conroe, The Woodlands) or Fort Bend County (Sugar Land, Pearland).
  • HOA prevalence: Many Houston-area neighborhoods have HOAs, which add closing costs through transfer fees and other charges.
