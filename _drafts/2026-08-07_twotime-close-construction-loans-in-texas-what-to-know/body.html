What Is a Two-Time Close Construction Loan?

A Two-Time Close construction loan is exactly what the name suggests: you'll close on two separate loans during your building journey.

The First Close: Construction Financing

When you're ready to break ground, you'll close on the construction loan. This is a short-term loan designed to cover all the costs of building your home—from land acquisition through the final walkthrough. During the construction phase, you'll typically make interest-only payments on the funds that have been drawn.

The Second Close: The Permanent Loan

Once your home is substantially complete (usually around 90% finished), you'll close on a permanent mortgage. This permanent loan pays off the construction loan and becomes your long-term financing for the home. The permanent loan is what you'll use to pay off the remainder of the construction balance and then make regular mortgage payments on.

This two-step process allows flexibility during construction while protecting both the lender and the borrower.

Why Use a Two-Time Close Structure?

Two-Time Close construction loans offer several practical advantages for Texas homeowners and builders:

  • Flexibility in financing terms: Your construction loan and permanent loan can have different terms, rates, and structures tailored to each phase.
  • Interest-only payments during build: You're not locked into a 30-year payment schedule while the home is still under construction.
  • Clear cost transparency: Draws are made as construction milestones are reached, so you can track exactly where your money is going.
  • Inspections and quality control: Third-party inspections happen at each draw stage, protecting your investment.
  • Time to make permanent financing decisions: You have time between the first and second close to lock in your long-term mortgage terms.

Key Requirements for Two-Time Close Construction Loans in Texas

Credit and Financial Standards

Lenders typically require a minimum FICO score of 720 for ground-up construction loans. You'll also need to demonstrate sufficient income and financial reserves. The exact requirements vary depending on your lender and loan program, but expect the underwriting process to be thorough.

Down Payment and Equity

Construction loans generally require a down payment, often ranging from 10% to 25% of the total project cost. Your lender will want to see that you have "skin in the game" as construction progresses.

Builder Experience and Credentials

Your builder's experience and track record matter significantly. Lenders want to see that your builder has successfully completed similar projects and maintains proper licensing and insurance.

Construction Plans and Budget

You'll need detailed construction plans, specifications, and a realistic budget. Your lender will want a clear cost breakdown showing how funds will be allocated throughout each phase of construction.

The Construction Loan Timeline

Understanding the timeline helps you plan your finances and move-in date realistically.

  • Application and approval: 2–4 weeks, depending on completeness of documentation
  • Underwriting: 1–2 weeks after submission of full application
  • Clear-to-close (first close): 3–5 days
  • Construction phase: Typically 6–12 months, depending on home complexity and size
  • Final inspection and preparation for second close: 2–4 weeks before completion
  • Permanent loan approval and second close: 1–2 weeks before or at substantial completion

The entire process from initial application to moving into your finished home usually spans 9 to 14 months.

Draw Process and Inspections

Here's how money flows during construction:

  1. Your builder submits a draw request with proof of completed work and invoices.
  2. The lender's inspector visits the site to verify progress matches the request.
  3. Once approved, funds are disbursed to pay contractors, suppliers, and subcontractors.
  4. You pay interest only on the amount drawn so far (unless otherwise agreed).
  5. This cycle repeats for each phase of construction until the home is complete.

This structured approach protects your investment and ensures quality throughout the build process.

Transitioning to Your Permanent Loan

As your home nears completion, Tyler Henschel and the CMG Home Loans team will help you prepare for the permanent loan process. You'll need to:

  • Complete a new loan application for the permanent mortgage
  • Provide updated financial documentation (recent pay stubs, tax returns, etc.)
  • Have the home appraised at its completed value
  • Obtain a final inspection and certificate of occupancy
  • Finalize your permanent loan terms before the second close

The good news? Because you've been working with your lender throughout construction, the permanent loan process is typically smoother and faster than a traditional mortgage application.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

When pursuing a Two-Time Close construction loan in the Houston area—from Cypress and Pearland to Conroe and beyond—avoid these pitfalls:

  • Underestimating the budget: Construction costs can rise unexpectedly. Build in a contingency reserve.
  • Changing plans mid-construction: Major changes can delay timelines and increase costs significantly.
  • Missing communication with your lender: Stay in touch and keep your lender informed of any changes or delays.
  • Neglecting the permanent loan process: Don't wait until the last minute to prepare for your second close.
  • Overlooking builder credentials: Verify your builder's licensing, insurance, and track record before signing anything.
