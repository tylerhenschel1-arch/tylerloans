How to Qualify for an AIO Loan in Texas

If you're a homeowner in the Houston metro area—whether you're in Tomball, Pearland, Sugar Land, The Woodlands, or anywhere else across the greater region—an All-In-One (AIO) loan could be a powerful financial tool to help you access your home's equity and simplify your finances.

But what does it take to qualify? Understanding the eligibility criteria ahead of time can help you prepare and move through the application process smoothly. In this guide, we'll walk you through the key requirements for AIO loan qualification in Texas, so you know exactly what lenders are looking for.

What Is an All-In-One Loan?

Before diving into qualification requirements, let's clarify what an AIO loan actually is. An All-In-One loan is a 30-year revolving line of credit secured by your primary residence or second home. Unlike traditional home equity lines of credit (HELOCs), the AIO doesn't have a separate draw period followed by a repayment period. Instead, interest accrues on your daily net balance, giving you flexibility to borrow, repay, and re-borrow as needed throughout the loan's term.

This makes the AIO particularly appealing for homeowners who want liquidity and flexibility while consolidating their borrowing into a single account.

Basic Eligibility Requirements for Texas Homeowners

To qualify for an AIO loan in Texas, you'll need to meet several foundational requirements:

  • Property Type: Your home must be a primary residence or a second home. Investment properties and rental homes are not eligible for AIO financing.
  • Equity Position: You'll need to have built up meaningful equity in your home. Texas home equity law allows cash-out loans up to 80% of your home's current value, though this is subject to other loan-to-value (LTV) considerations and your lender's specific guidelines.
  • Texas Residency & Cooling-Off Period: As a Texas homeowner, you're protected by state home equity laws. These include a mandatory 12-day cooling-off period before your loan can close, and any home equity loan requires at least 1 year to have passed since your most recent prior equity loan. Your lender will provide required disclosures in both English and Spanish.
  • Proof of Occupancy: You must occupy the property as your primary residence (or be able to document it as your legitimate second home).

Credit Score Requirements

Your credit score is one of the first things lenders evaluate. For an AIO loan, most lenders—including CMG Home Loans—typically require a minimum credit score in the mid-to-upper 600s, though a stronger score (700 or higher) will generally give you better terms and options.

Your credit report will also be reviewed for:

  • Recent late payments or delinquencies
  • Collections accounts or charge-offs
  • Overall payment history and credit utilization
  • The age and variety of your credit accounts

If your credit score is lower than you'd like, addressing any obvious issues before applying can improve your chances and strengthen your overall application.

Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI)

Lenders use your debt-to-income ratio to determine how much you can safely borrow relative to your monthly income. Your DTI is calculated by dividing your total monthly debt obligations (mortgage, car loans, credit cards, student loans, personal loans, and the new AIO payment) by your gross monthly income.

For AIO loans, conventional underwriting standards typically allow DTI ratios up to the mid-to-upper 40s or low 50s, depending on other compensating factors in your application. However, your specific limit may vary based on your credit profile, assets, employment history, and your lender's underwriting guidelines.

To strengthen your DTI:

  • Pay down existing revolving debt (credit cards, lines of credit)
  • Avoid opening new credit accounts right before applying
  • Document all sources of income clearly
  • Consider whether you can pay off small debts to lower your overall monthly obligations

Employment & Income Verification

Lenders need to verify that you have stable, verifiable income to support your new loan obligation. You'll typically need to provide:

  • Recent pay stubs (usually the last 2 months)
  • W-2s or tax returns from the past 2 years
  • Written verification of employment from your employer
  • Bank statements showing direct deposits

If you're self-employed, you may need additional documentation, such as business tax returns, profit-and-loss statements, or business bank statements. The goal is to demonstrate consistent, reliable income that will support your new loan payment.

Asset & Reserve Requirements

Lenders also want to see that you have sufficient liquid assets (savings, checking accounts, money market accounts, etc.) to cover a few months of mortgage and living expenses. This demonstrates financial stability and gives the lender confidence that you can handle your obligations even if your income is temporarily disrupted.

The specific reserve requirements can vary by lender and loan program, but having 2–6 months of reserves in liquid form is often viewed favorably.

Property Value & Appraisal

To determine how much equity you have available to borrow, your lender will order an appraisal of your home. This appraisal establishes the current market value and, combined with your outstanding mortgage balance, determines your equity position and maximum borrowing amount.

Keep in mind that the appraisal will also assess the general condition of the property. Major defects or needed repairs can affect the appraised value and, in some cases, may require you to address issues before closing.

Documentation & Disclosures

Once you've met the basic criteria, you'll work through the formal application process. This includes:

  • Completing a full loan application with accurate personal and financial information
  • Authorizing a credit check and background verification
  • Providing all required financial documentation (pay stubs, tax returns, bank statements, etc.)
  • Receiving and reviewing loan estimate and other federally required disclosures
  • Working with a title company to confirm your ownership and lien position
  • Complying with all Texas-specific home equity protections and disclosures
