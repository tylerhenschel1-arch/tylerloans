How to read your Texas property tax bill correctly

By Tyler Henschel · July 01, 2026 · Houston, TX

How to Read Your Texas Property Tax Bill Correctly

If you're a homeowner in the Greater Houston area—whether you're in Tomball, Sugar Land, Pearland, or anywhere across the Houston metro—you've likely received a property tax bill at least once. And if you're like most people, you probably wondered what all those numbers and abbreviations actually mean.

Texas property tax bills can look confusing at first glance, but once you understand the key sections and line items, they become much more manageable. In this guide, we'll walk through what you need to know to read your Texas property tax bill with confidence.

Why Understanding Your Property Tax Bill Matters

Your property tax bill is one of the most important financial documents you'll receive as a homeowner. It tells you:

  • How much you owe in property taxes each year
  • Who is taxing you (school district, county, city, special districts)
  • What your property is assessed at
  • Your homestead exemption status
  • Whether there are any errors in your assessment

If you're planning to refinance or take out a home equity loan—or if you're simply trying to budget for homeownership costs—knowing how to read this bill is essential. When Tyler Henschel, Senior Loan Officer at CMG Home Loans, works with clients throughout the Houston metro, he often finds that homeowners don't fully understand their property tax obligations until they're deep in the mortgage or refinance process.

The Main Sections of Your Texas Property Tax Bill

1. Property Identification Information

At the top of your bill, you'll see your property address, account number, and parcel number. Double-check this section for accuracy. If the address is wrong or the property description doesn't match your home, contact your local appraisal district immediately. These errors can affect your loan documents, insurance, and title work.

2. Assessment Information

This section shows the appraised value of your home and any applicable exemptions. You'll typically see:

  • Appraised Value: What the county appraisal district says your home is worth
  • Assessed Value: The value used to calculate your taxes (usually the same as appraised value, but reduced by exemptions)
  • Homestead Exemption: If you claim this exemption (available to Texas primary residence owners), a portion of your home's value is protected from taxation

The homestead exemption is significant for Houston-area homeowners in communities like The Woodlands, Cypress, and Bellaire. In Texas, a standard homestead exemption reduces your assessed value by $25,000 of the home's value (though some districts offer additional exemptions for seniors or disabled homeowners).

3. Tax Calculation Breakdown

This is where you'll see the actual dollars owed. Your bill will list each taxing entity separately:

  • School District: Usually the largest portion of your bill
  • County: Used for county services
  • City or Municipality: For city services (if applicable)
  • Special Districts: May include hospital districts, water districts, or emergency services

Each entity has its own tax rate (expressed as a dollar amount per $100 of assessed value). For example, if your assessed value is $400,000 and the school district rate is $1.06 per $100, you'd owe $4,240 to the school district alone.

4. Payment Information and Due Dates

Texas property taxes are typically due by January 31st each year. However, they're usually billed in October and can be paid in two installments (October 1 – November 30 for the first half, December 1 – January 31 for the second half).

Your bill will show:

  • Total amount due
  • Due date
  • Payment instructions
  • Penalties for late payment (typically 7% if paid after January 31st)

Common Abbreviations and Terms You'll See

  • CAD (Central Appraisal District): The county office that assesses property values
  • HE (Homestead Exemption): Your property tax break as a primary resident
  • TCEQ: Texas Commission on Environmental Quality
  • MUD: Municipal Utility District (common in the Katy, Spring, and Conroe areas)
  • Tax Rate per $100: How the taxing entity calculates your obligation based on your home's value

How to Verify Your Bill Is Correct

Before you pay, take a few minutes to verify accuracy:

  • Check the property description: Does it match your home (lot size, square footage, number of bedrooms)?
  • Confirm your homestead exemption status: If you claimed one, it should appear on the bill
  • Review the appraised value: If it seems significantly higher than comparable homes in your Friendswood, Memorial, or other Houston-area neighborhood, you may want to file a protest
  • Verify all taxing entities: Make sure you're being taxed by the correct school district, city, and special districts

If you find an error, Texas law allows you to file a formal protest with your local appraisal district. The deadline is typically around May 15th each year.

How Property Taxes Affect Your Mortgage and Refinance

When you're applying for a mortgage or refinancing, lenders like Tyler Henschel at CMG Home Loans use your property tax bill to estimate your total monthly housing costs. Property taxes are a major component of your PITI (Principal, Interest, Taxes, and Insurance), which directly impacts your debt-to-income ratio and loan eligibility.

If your property taxes are higher than expected, it can affect:

  • Your monthly mortgage payment estimate
  • Your debt-to-income calculation
  • The loan amount you qualify for

Having an accurate understanding of your property tax bill helps you budget more effectively and sets realistic expectations when working with a loan officer.

Key Takeaways

Reading your Texas property tax bill doesn't have to be stressful. Focus on these key elements:

  • Verify your property identification and assessed value
  • Understand which entities are taxing you and at what rate
  • Confirm your homestead exemption is applied
  • Know your payment deadline and amount
  • File a protest if you believe your assessment is incorrect

Whether you're a new homeowner in Heights or have owned property across the Houston metro for years, staying informed about your property tax bill helps you make better financial decisions and avoid surprises.
