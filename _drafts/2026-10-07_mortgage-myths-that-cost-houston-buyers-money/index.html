Mortgage Myths That Cost Houston Buyers Money

Buying a home in the Houston metro area is one of the biggest financial decisions you'll make. From Tomball to Pearland, from Memorial to Magnolia, thousands of families are pursuing the dream of homeownership every year. But along the way, many fall victim to persistent mortgage myths—misconceptions that can cost them thousands of dollars or derail their dreams entirely.

As a Senior Loan Officer serving the Greater Houston area, I've heard these myths repeated countless times. In this post, I'm breaking down the most damaging falsehoods so you can move forward with confidence and accurate information.

Myth #1: You Need 20% Down to Buy a Home

This is perhaps the most pervasive myth in homebuying, and it stops many qualified buyers before they even start looking.

The truth? You don't need 20% down. Depending on the loan program and your situation, you may qualify with significantly less. Conventional loans often accept down payments as low as 3–5%. FHA loans, popular among first-time buyers throughout the Houston area, can work with down payments of 3.5%. VA loans and USDA loans may require no down payment at all for eligible borrowers.

The catch: a lower down payment typically means additional costs like mortgage insurance or higher overall interest charges over the life of the loan. But the point stands—the 20% rule is a myth. If you're currently renting in Conroe or Friendswood and thought you couldn't afford to buy, this misconception might have been holding you back unfairly.

Myth #2: Your Credit Score Needs to Be Perfect

Another myth: you need a 750+ credit score (or higher) to qualify for a mortgage.

While a higher credit score certainly helps you access better terms and pricing, you don't need a perfect score. Many lenders, including CMG Home Loans, work with borrowers across a wide range of credit profiles. Conventional loans may work with scores in the 620–640 range depending on other compensating factors. FHA loans often accommodate scores in the low 600s.

What matters more than a single number is the overall picture: your income stability, debt-to-income ratio, employment history, and the reason for any past credit challenges. A financial hiccup from five years ago doesn't automatically disqualify you from homeownership in Spring, Sugar Land, or anywhere else in the Greater Houston area.

Myth #3: Getting Pre-Approved Locks in Your Interest Rate

Many buyers think that once they receive a pre-approval letter, their interest rate is locked in—and they're surprised when their loan officer mentions that rates fluctuate.

Pre-approval is a crucial step, but it's not a rate guarantee. A pre-approval letter indicates that a lender has reviewed your financial profile and believes you're a qualified borrower. It does not mean your rate is set in stone. Rates change daily based on market conditions. When you're ready to move forward with a specific property and formally apply, you'll have the opportunity to lock your rate at that time.

This distinction is important because it affects your strategy. Don't delay the homebuying process thinking your rate is already secured. Talk to your loan officer about the right time to lock in, especially in a market where rates are moving.

Myth #4: You Can't Get a Mortgage If You're Self-Employed

Self-employed professionals and business owners throughout the Houston metro—in Bellaire, The Woodlands, Cypress, and beyond—often believe they can't qualify for a mortgage. This myth has cost many entrepreneurs their shot at homeownership.

The reality: self-employment is not a barrier. Lenders understand that self-employed income looks different from W-2 income, and loan programs have been designed to accommodate it. You'll typically need to provide additional documentation, such as tax returns, profit-and-loss statements, and bank statements, to verify income stability. But qualification is entirely possible.

In fact, many lenders have specialized programs and expertise for self-employed borrowers. Tyler Henschel and the team at CMG Home Loans regularly work with entrepreneurs and business owners throughout the Greater Houston area to structure financing that works for their unique situations.

Myth #5: Having Debt Automatically Disqualifies You

Many prospective buyers assume that carrying credit card balances, car loans, or student loans means they can't get approved for a mortgage.

The truth is more nuanced. Lenders look at your debt-to-income ratio—the percentage of your gross monthly income that goes toward debt payments. Most conventional loan programs work comfortably with borrowers in the 43–50% debt-to-income range, depending on the lender's guidelines and automated underwriting system. Some loan programs can accommodate higher ratios when compensating factors are strong.

The presence of debt doesn't disqualify you; what matters is whether your income is sufficient to support both existing debt and a new mortgage payment. If you're worried about your debt levels, a conversation with an experienced loan officer can clarify where you actually stand.

Myth #6: Mortgage Shopping Tanks Your Credit Score

Many buyers avoid shopping around with multiple lenders because they've heard that each application will ding their credit. This myth actually costs buyers money by discouraging them from finding the best loan terms.

Here's what happens: when you apply for a mortgage, the lender pulls a hard inquiry, which does show up on your credit report. However, the credit bureaus understand that homebuyers shop around. Multiple mortgage inquiries within a 14–45 day window (depending on the scoring model) typically count as a single inquiry for credit-scoring purposes. You can—and should—shop with different lenders without fear of cumulative credit damage.

This is how you find competitive rates and terms. Don't let this myth prevent you from exploring your options.

Myth #7: You Need Perfect Employment History

Job changes, career transitions, and periods of unemployment can trigger anxiety for homebuyers. The myth is that any employment gap or job change will disqualify you.

Lenders want to see stable income and a reasonable employment history, but it's not black and white. A recent job change to a higher-paying position in your field is generally viewed favorably. A brief gap explained by education or relocation is understandable. What lenders scrutinize more closely is erratic or declining income or a pattern of frequent, unexplained job changes.

Your unique situation matters. That's why speaking directly with a knowledgeable loan officer—rather than relying on myths you've heard—is so valuable.
