Construction Loan Draws Explained for Houston Builders

If you're a builder working across the Greater Houston area—whether you're developing in Sugar Land, Tomball, The Woodlands, or Cypress—you've probably heard the term "construction loan draw" thrown around. But what does it really mean, and how does the process actually work? Understanding construction loan draws is critical to managing your project timeline and cash flow effectively. Let's break it down.

What Is a Construction Loan Draw?

A construction loan draw is a disbursement of funds from your lender that you receive as your project progresses. Unlike a traditional mortgage where you receive one lump sum at closing, a construction loan releases money in stages—typically tied to specific milestones or phases of construction.

Think of it this way: you're not getting all your money upfront. Instead, your lender releases funds based on the work completed, inspections passed, and the current state of your project. This protects both you and the lender by ensuring that money goes directly toward actual construction rather than sitting idle.

Why Draws Matter for Your Project

Construction projects require significant capital, and draws allow you to manage that cash flow intelligently. Rather than carrying the entire project cost on your balance sheet from day one, you're accessing funds as you need them. This approach:

  • Preserves working capital for other business needs
  • Aligns funding with actual construction progress
  • Reduces the amount of interest you pay (since you're not borrowing money you're not yet using)
  • Provides a built-in checkpoint system to verify quality and progress

How the Draw Process Typically Works

The draw process generally follows these steps:

  1. Initial Inspection: Before releasing the first draw, your lender's inspector visits the site to verify that work has begun and meets the loan specifications.
  2. Request Submission: You or your contractor submit a draw request, often accompanied by photos, invoices, and documentation of work completed.
  3. Lender Inspection: The lender sends an inspector to verify the work matches what was requested and that the project is progressing according to plan.
  4. Approval and Funding: If everything checks out, the lender approves the draw and disburses funds—typically within 5–10 business days.
  5. Repeat: This cycle repeats throughout construction until the project reaches completion.

For builders in neighborhoods like Memorial, Heights, Bellaire, or expanding areas like Magnolia and Conroe, this process ensures accountability and gives lenders confidence that the project is moving forward as promised.

Common Draw Schedules

Draw schedules vary, but most construction loans use one of two approaches:

Percentage-Based Draws: Funds are released as a percentage of total project completion. For example, you might receive 20% of the loan at framing, 30% at roof completion, and so on.

Milestone-Based Draws: Money is released when specific construction milestones are reached—foundation complete, framing complete, mechanicals rough-in, etc. This approach is common for ground-up construction in the Houston metro and works well for projects in areas like Pearland, Spring, and Friendswood.

The Role of Your Lender's Inspector

Your lender will assign an inspector who becomes a key player in the draw process. This person verifies that:

  • Work performed matches the submitted draw request
  • Quality meets building codes and loan requirements
  • No liens or other issues have arisen
  • The project remains on schedule

While inspections can feel like oversight, they're actually protecting your interests too. A lender inspector catches problems early, preventing costly issues down the road.

Managing Your Draw Strategy

Successful builders coordinate closely with their lender from the start. Here's what that looks like:

  • Pre-Construction Planning: Work with your lender to establish a detailed draw schedule before breaking ground.
  • Documentation: Keep meticulous records of all work, expenses, and invoices. Photos dated and labeled are invaluable.
  • Timing: Submit draw requests with enough lead time to account for inspection scheduling and approval windows.
  • Communication: Maintain regular contact with your lender to address questions before they become problems.

Typical Timeline for Draws

While every project is unique, ground-up construction in the Greater Houston area typically sees draws every 4–8 weeks, corresponding to major construction phases. A typical single-family home project might include:

  • Draw 1: Foundation and site work
  • Draw 2: Framing and exterior
  • Draw 3: Roof and weatherproofing
  • Draw 4: Mechanical, electrical, and plumbing rough-in
  • Draw 5: Drywall and interior finishing
  • Draw 6: Final touches and completion

Renovation projects, which are common throughout Cypress, Tomball, and other established Houston neighborhoods, may follow a slightly different schedule based on the scope of work.

What Happens at Final Draw?

The final draw—often called the "takeout" or completion draw—is released once construction is substantially complete and your lender has confirmed that all work meets specifications. At this point, your construction loan typically converts to permanent financing if you haven't already arranged that separately.
